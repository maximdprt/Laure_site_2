Sometimes all it takes is walking into a room to feel that something calms us, or on the contrary unsettles us. A golden light at the end of the day, a deep blue wall, a bunch of orange flowers on a table: colours reach us before we even have time to think about them. But what do they say about us? And what might our attraction to some colours, or our rejection of others, reveal?

This article invites you to look at your relationship with colour in a new way. Not to put you in a box, but to open up a reflection on what you feel.

## Our intimate relationship with colour

We live surrounded by colour. We choose it for our clothes, our homes, our everyday objects. Often, we don't really know why. We simply say: "I love this green", "I never wear yellow", "This red makes me uneasy".

These preferences are not fixed. They shift with the seasons of life. A colour that drew you in at twenty may leave you indifferent today. A shade you avoided for years may suddenly feel right, almost necessary. These movements are interesting: they often accompany inner changes, periods of transition, new needs.

### A personal relationship above all

There are many cultural associations around colour. Blue is often linked with calm, red with energy, green with nature. These references speak to many people, but they remain general. Your own relationship with a colour depends on your story, your memories and how you feel at the moment.

> The same colour can soothe one person and unsettle another.

It is precisely this personal dimension that makes the question so fascinating. What matters is not what a colour "means" in absolute terms, but how it makes you feel, today.

## Attraction, indifference, rejection

Faced with a palette of colours, we all have three main kinds of reaction.

### Attraction

Some colours draw us in immediately. We want to look at them, to surround ourselves with them. This attraction can reflect a present need, a resource we would like to lean on, or a part of ourselves we recognise and like.

### Indifference

Other colours leave us neutral. We see them without really noticing them. This indifference is not meaningless, though: it may point to areas of our life that are not, at the moment, at the centre of our concerns.

### Rejection

Finally, some colours provoke a rejection, sometimes a very clear one. We find them unpleasant, garish, sad. This rejection is often the richest source of insight. It may signal a tension, a part of ourselves we find hard to welcome, or an emotional area that needs attention.

There is no right or wrong reaction. Attraction is not "better" than rejection. Together, all these responses form a picture, a snapshot of your personal balance at this moment.

## What colour can open up

Observing your reactions to colour is a little like holding up a mirror. Elements appear that you may not have put into words.

Your relationship with colour can open up a reflection on:

- your current emotions;
- your needs, sometimes set aside;
- your personal resources, the ones you can rely on;
- certain blocks or tensions;
- the way you function day to day;
- your aspirations and your wish for change.

The value of this approach is that it goes through feeling rather than analysis. You don't need to be able to explain what you are going through to choose a colour. The choice is spontaneous, and that spontaneity is exactly what makes it precious.

### A starting point, not a conclusion

A colour never says everything about you. It opens a door; it invites conversation. It is in the exchange, by putting words to what emerges, that the reflection takes on its full meaning. Colours then become a way to understand yourself better, without judgement and without labels.
